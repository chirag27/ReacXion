# Lal Kitab: Pakka Ghar, States, and Rinas (original summary)

Lal Kitab is its own paradigm and is not read with Parashari rules. It uses a
fixed grid in which the houses are tied to signs — the first house is always
Aries, the second Taurus, and so on — so a planet's house is given by the sign
it occupies. (This summary follows the commonly reproduced 1941 edition.)

Each planet has a pakka ghar (permanent house) where it is considered settled
and strong. A planet also has a working state: it is awakened when it shares a
house with another planet, asleep when it sits alone but is aspected, and blind
when it is alone and unaspected. An awakened planet gives active results; a
sleeping or blind planet needs a trigger — often a remedy — to express itself.

Rinas are ancestral debts read from afflicted placements. The principal debts
are Pitra Rin (debt of ancestors and father, linked to the 9th house and an
afflicted Sun), Matri Rin (mother, the 4th house and the Moon), Stri Rin (spouse
and women, the 7th house and Venus), Bahin-Beti Rin (sisters and daughters, the
6th house and Mercury), Santan Rin (progeny, the 5th house), and Atma Rin (the
self, the 1st house). A debt marks a life area under strain that the chart asks
the native to repay.

Remedies (upay or totkay) are simple, symbolic acts keyed to the afflicted
planet or the detected debt. They are the heart of Lal Kitab practice: instead
of only describing a difficulty, the system prescribes a concrete corrective
action aimed at the planet or ancestor concerned.
